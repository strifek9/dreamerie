# Dreamerie card creation guide

Owner direction: elegant, beautiful and whimsical, but also fantastical, weird, sometimes abstract and emotionally varied. A dream need not make literal sense. This guide applies to future normal cards, holiday cards and their counterparts, and supersedes earlier habits of repeating cozy nature scenes.

## Variety is a requirement

- Vary the SUBJECT, not merely its color or holiday accessories. Robots, aliens, mermaids, ninjas, cowboys, computers, monsters, people, animals, machines and inanimate objects can all belong here. These are examples, not a checklist or a new set of motifs to repeat forever.
- Mix worlds in surprising ways: a computer that dreams in spaghetti, a cowboy lassoing a doorway, a mermaid swimming through a television, a robot misplacing its shadow. Dream logic should affect the scene, not just put a normal subject in front of a magical background.
- Flowers, moons, birds, stars, clouds and forests remain welcome, but must not become the automatic vocabulary of every image. Do not default every character to a cute woodland animal or every scene to a golden sunset.
- Vary the emotional experience: joy, tenderness, wonder, silliness, mystery, loneliness, melancholy, frustration, anger, suspense and fear. Anger can be absurd or expressive; scary can be uncanny or spooky. Not every card must feel happy or cozy.
- Keep everything PG: no gore, graphic injury, torture, sexual imagery, hateful stereotypes or disturbing body horror. Monsters can be unsettling without graphic violence. No jump-scare design.
- Vary palette, lighting, setting, scale, composition, density and visual rhythm. Use some calm/open scenes and some energetic/busy ones. Do not turn stylistic consistency into identical scenery.

## Keep Dreamerie recognizable

Use the existing hand-painted illustration quality, textured gouache, visible brushwork, elegant color and inspectable native detail. Avoid photorealism, plastic 3D rendering, generic greeting-card art and randomly pasted props. Abstract does not mean muddy or unreadable: use impossible scale, metamorphosis, contradictory shadows, fragmented spaces and associative visual storytelling while keeping distinct objects and meaningful detail.

Holiday identity should be woven into a strange original dream, not a literal greeting card. Keep Memorial Day, Veterans Day and other civic observances respectful; their symbolism need not be festive. Avoid cultural caricatures, borrowed sacred motifs and historical reenactments used as generic decoration.

## Plan a varied batch before generating

For each card record its occasion (if any), core subject, impossible premise, mood and palette. Compare neighboring entries. If several reuse the same main subject, setting, lighting or emotional tone, revise them before generation. A useful mix includes organic, mechanical, human, fantastical and object-led compositions. These are editorial checks, not rigid quotas.

Before every generation batch, read this guide and inspect representative existing cards for their brushwork and quality—not as compositions to copy. Carry the chosen subject, impossible premise, mood, palette and PG limits into each generation prompt; do not rely on a previous conversation being available. Check variety against the existing collection as well as within the new batch. A beautiful card can be eerie, angry or melancholy without becoming visually harsh or losing Dreamerie's identity.

## A painting must also be a fair puzzle

- Preserve high-resolution landscape artwork (currently 1672 × 941 or greater with matching aspect) for zoom and responsive comparison.
- Author exactly five real scene-fitting changes, from recognizable to subtle. Vary change types: shape, count, missing detail, mechanism, pattern, meaningful object color or replacement. Avoid five arbitrary discolorations or the same red bow/gold buttons in every card.
- Keep clue regions separate, visible and inspectable. Do not make randomness or visual noise the source of difficulty. Both images must retain the same art direction and composition.
- Use the image-generation skill for raster creation/edits. Inspect each original before requesting its counterpart, then inspect the actual five-region playable composite and all five paired answer crops. Do not assume generated edits obeyed the prompt.
- Update image paths, hit regions, labels, title and poem together. No stale coordinates after a composition change. Keep normal saved/published cards stable; consider saved-attempt compatibility before replacing published art.
- Poems remain brief, original, clearly rhyming and allude to shifting dreams without spoiling answers. Record exact prompts and source provenance.

Keep the interface and rules simple. Richness belongs in the artwork, not extra modes, ornament layers or complicated scoring.
